# Definitions

> Information system: set of elements collecting, processing, storing, and distributing information, to support the activities of decision-making and control of an enterprise   
> In addition to supporting decision-making, information systems can help managers and employees to analyse problems, visualise complex situations and design new products   
> Information systems contain information about people, places and objects in the company

> Data are simple facts representing events occurring inside or outside the company, they are encoded in numbers, images, sounds; however, such facts need to be structured and interpreted   
> Information is data transformed into a meaningful and useful form

# Characteristics of information

> Information is used to decide how to achieve business goals   
> It is necessary to define the processes for collecting information to plan,organize and control company activities   
> Each activity produces information   
> Information is intangible assets   
> Information is not destroyed when it is used, on the contrary it can produce new information

# Wrong assumptions

> The information system is by no means the domain of the IT manager alone   
> Any role contributes to the improvement of information management   
Despite this   
> The concept of information system is often associated exclusively with hardware and software tools (the IT system)   
> In many companies, the person responsible for the information system is the one managing the data processing center

# Benefits of information systems

> Enterprises invest in information systems because they represent economic value for the company   

Strategic benefits

> Reactivity and responsiveness to market variables   
> Flow control, cost control, technological adaptation,，.

Operational benefits

> Shared, pervasive, updated, organized information flow   
> Rationalization of information and business processes, reduction of intraand inter-company coordination costs,...

# Components of the enterprise system

Enterprise system

> Characterized by
> One or more institutional goals or objectives   
> A structure, i.e. a set of parts, subsets, resources   
> The processes, i.e. the activities that occur in the structure

Organizational system

> Characterized by
> Resources (people, money, materials, information)   
> Rules that allow the rationalization of the company's activity through the coordinated use of resources

Information system

> Combination of resources and organized procedures for the collection, storage, processing and transmission of information necessary to carry out of the company's activities (operational, management, planning,control and evaluation of the organization)

IT system

> Set of IT tools used for the automatic processing of an organization's information in order to facilitate the functions of its information system

# Information system and organization

Given the close relationship between the information system and the organization,on what basis is it possible to develop the information system best suited to the company's needs?

> Representation by function: each business function has its own Information System (e.g.: IS for marketing, IS for administration, IS for production)
> Representation by activity level: each type of activity (classified according to the Anthony's pyramid) has its own information system
> Representation by process: each business process has its own information system (e.g.: IS for the launch of a new product, IS for the acquisition of a new resource)

# Representation by activity levels

Corporate activities are divided into three levels

1. Activities at the strategy level (corporate mission): involve long-term planning (organizational and investment decisions)   
2. Activities at the tactic level: involve planning the available resources and monitoring the achievement of results in terms of effectiveness and efficiency   
3. Activies at the operational level: concern the day-to-day life of the business

The activity-based information system is characterised by the fact that it is designed for the needs of the level where the activities are located

# Representation by process

### Process Diagram

The image illustrates the fundamental components of a process flow, which is a sequence of activities that transforms inputs into a specific output.

*   **Input**: This represents the resources, materials, or data that enter the system to initiate the transformation.
*   **Process (Activities)**: A sequence of three interconnected "Activity" blocks where the actual transformation or value-added work takes place.
*   **Output**: The final result, product, or service generated at the end of the activity sequence.

From an information point of view, process representation has the advantage of showing which information "flows" through the organization

The information system by process is characterised by being designed to meet the concept of integration between business functions

# ERP System Definition

An Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) is an IT system for the integrated management of relevant business processes   
It guides and supports the company in its business operations   
With the rise in popularity of ERP and the reduction of ICT costs, applications have been developed to help companies implement this approach in day-to-day activities such as inventory control，order tracking, customer services, finance and human resources

# Evolution of the ERP Information system

1960 - Birth of MRP (Material Requirements Planning)

> Need to manage the supply of raw materials and balance production with customer demand. First calculation software to plan production, material procurement and product delivery.   
> By using such systems,companies were able to keep their inventory levels low, which in turn led to aconsequent reduction in money invested in inventory.

1970 - Consolidation of MRP and birth of the first software houses

> In 1975，MRP software was operating in 70o companies and running on huge mainframe computers that didn't even have the computing power of today's laptops. In 1972, the first data-processing software development company was founded to create business software that ran in real time.

1980 - Evolution of MRP: MRP II

> In the 1980s, MRP technology underwent several developments that increased its capacity and power,turning it into what many called "MRP I" or "Manufacturing Resource Planning".The implementation of this technology made it possible to manage multiple production processes by increasing the data storage capacity of the devices.

1990 - Merging of MRP and MRP I: ERP software

> The acronym ERP was first used in the 1990s. It described software that combined the functions of its MRP and MRP Il predecessors and included additional 'back office' and business management functions. Thus, elements of companies such as finance and accounting, human resources， project management and warehouse management were managed solely with one software.

2000 - Changes introduced by ERP II

> ERP I was a software that could also coordinate with systems outside the company. This involved the integration of supply chain management， customer relationship management (CRM) and business intelligence.

Today - ERP, a constantly evolving software

> Nowadays, ERP systems are even more advanced and evolving. Many ERP suites are based on cloud technology and have remote web access via advanced applications that can also be used on mobile devices.

### Information Flow and Reporting Process

The image illustrates the flow of information from an initial order arrival through various departments to final budget reporting.

#### 1. Initiation Phase
*   **Order Arrival**: The process is triggered by an incoming order, represented by a figure making a call.
*   **Order Report**: This activity generates a formal "Order Report" documentation.

#### 2. Parallel Operations
Once the order arrives, information flows into two distinct functional paths:
*   **Administrative Path**: The Order Report is sent directly to the **Administration** team for processing.
*   **Logistics Path**: The order notification also reaches the **Warehouse**, where a physical check or inventory action occurs.

#### 3. Documentation and Consolidation
*   **Warehouse Report**: Following the warehouse activities, a specific "Warehouse Report" is generated.
*   **Administrative Processing**: The Administration department receives both the initial **Order Report** and the subsequent **Warehouse Report**.
*   **Budget Report**: After consolidating the information from both sources, the Administration team produces the final **Budget Report**.

# Centralised architecture (ERP)

### Integrated Information System (ERP Concept)

The image illustrates a centralized approach to data management, often associated with Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems. Unlike the previous fragmented process, this model utilizes a single source of truth.

#### 1. Centralized Core
*   **Unified System**: Represented by the central cylinder, this acts as a shared database where all information is stored, updated, and accessed in real-time by different departments.

#### 2. Functional Integration
Four key business functions are shown feeding information directly into (and potentially pulling from) the unified system:
*   **Order Arrival**: Data regarding new customer orders is immediately captured.
*   **Invoicing**: Billing information is generated based on the actual order data stored in the system.
*   **Warehouse**: Inventory levels and shipping status are updated automatically.
*   **Revenue Recording**: Financial data and growth metrics are tracked based on real-time transactional information.

#### 3. Technical Benefits
*   **Data Integrity**: Eliminates the need for multiple manual reports (like the Order or Warehouse reports seen previously), reducing the risk of human error and data duplication.
*   **Real-time Visibility**: Every department has access to the same updated information simultaneously, facilitating faster decision-making.
*   **Process Efficiency**: Automates the flow of information between sales, logistics, and finance.

# PLM - Product Lifecycle Management

Process to manage the entire life cycle of a product: conception, requirements,design, prototyping, industrialisation,active life management, recovery and recycling management, end of life and disposal.

Not just an information technology, but rather an integrated approach, based on a set of technologies and methodologies for organising collaborative work and defining processes.

The goal of PLM is to optimise (less time,lower costs, higher quality, lower risks) the development, launch, modification and withdrawal of products or services from the market.

# MES - Manufacturing Execution System

Production control system: dispatch of orders, quantity and time progress, deposits in stock   
Data collected through direct connections to machines (PLC/Scada) or manual declarations of the working operators   
Information provided in real time to offices providing a complete view of the progress of orders, the physical status of resources and the materials used

### The Automation Pyramid (ISA-95 Model)

The image illustrates the hierarchy of control and management systems within an integrated industrial environment.

*   **Level 4: Enterprise Level (ERP)**: Handles enterprise resource planning, financial management, and high-level business planning.
*   **Level 3: Management Level (MES)**: Manages manufacturing execution on the shop floor, monitoring efficiency, quality, and performance.
*   **Level 2: Supervision Level (SCADA)**: Provides supervisory control of production lines and real-time data visualization for operators.
*   **Level 1: Control Level (PLC/PAC)**: Acts as the "brain" of the machinery, where programmed logic control cycles are executed.
*   **Level 0: Field Level (Sensors & Actuators)**: The physical level where sensors detect data and actuators (motors, valves) perform mechanical actions.

---

**Technical Note**: The dotted line between the control level and the field level often represents the boundary between Information Technology (IT) and Operational Technology (OT).

# ERP - PLM - MES

### Integrated Automation Architecture: PLM, ERP, and MES

The image illustrates the hierarchy of a "Totally Integrated Automation" system, highlighting the synergy between engineering, business planning, and shop-floor execution.

*   **PLM (Product Lifecycle Management)**: At the enterprise level, PLM manages **Product Data** (design and technical specifications).
*   **ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning)**: Also at the enterprise level, ERP handles **Production Planning**, logistics, and business-side resources.
*   **MES (Manufacturing Execution System)**: Occupies the **Management** level, bridging the gap between business planning (ERP/PLM) and physical operations. It monitors and executes production in real-time.
*   **Operations & Control**: These levels utilize an **Engineering Framework** and **SCADA** systems to supervise **Controllers**, **HMIs**, and **CNC** machines.
*   **Field Level**: The physical base where **Sensors**, **Actuators**, **Drive Systems**, and **Power Supplies** interact with the manufacturing process.
